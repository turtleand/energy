Static electricity begins when electric charge becomes imbalanced.

One region has more negative charge. Another region has more positive charge. That separation creates electrical tension between them.

The core model is:

```text
charge imbalance -> rising electrical tension -> sudden discharge
```

## The intuition

Static charge is like tension building before a snap.

As the imbalance grows, the system stores more electrical tension. At first nothing dramatic happens. The separated charge is just sitting there, held apart by the situation that created it.

But the imbalance cannot grow forever. When the tension becomes large enough for charges to cross the gap, the stored imbalance releases suddenly. That release is the spark.

A tiny shock from a doorknob and lightning are both examples of static discharge. The scale is different, but the lesson is the same: imbalance builds, reaches a limit, then releases.

## Buildup

Static charge buildup has three useful pieces:

1. **Imbalance**: charge is unevenly distributed.
2. **Electrical tension**: the separated charge creates a stronger field as the imbalance grows.
3. **Critical point**: once the gap can no longer hold the separation, discharge happens.

A simple mental model is:

```text
constant buildup -> larger imbalance -> critical point
```

The buildup can come from rubbing materials, contact and separation, clouds and air motion, or other charge-separating processes. The exact cause can change. The important structure is that charge separation increases over time.

## Discharge

Discharge is the sudden correction of the imbalance.

When the critical point is reached, charge moves across the gap. The spark is brief because the built-up difference is being reduced. After the discharge, the system is closer to balance, so the buildup can begin again.

```text
critical imbalance -> spark -> lower imbalance -> buildup restarts
```

## Final model

Static electricity is not about a steady working loop. It is about stored imbalance.

```text
charge becomes imbalanced,
the imbalance grows,
a critical point is reached,
the imbalance releases as a spark,
and the system resets.
```

This lesson is a model for understanding behavior, not a guide for working on electrical systems. Real electrical systems can be dangerous and should be handled with proper training, tools, and safety rules.
